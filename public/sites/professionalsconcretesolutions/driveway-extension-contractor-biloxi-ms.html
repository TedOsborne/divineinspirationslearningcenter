Professional Concrete Solutions is a driveway extension contractor in Biloxi, MS, helping homeowners and businesses add practical concrete space without making the addition look disconnected from the property. Extensions can create side-by-side parking, a parking pad, a wider entrance, better garage access, or dedicated space for a boat, trailer, recreational vehicle, or work truck.

  • Decide what will use the added space before setting its width and length.

When a concrete driveway extension makes sense

A driveway extension can solve a specific daily problem. Families may need room for another driver without blocking the main driveway. A property owner may want to keep a boat or trailer off the lawn. A business may need a cleaner approach, an additional parking area, or more room for service vehicles. In each case, the purpose should determine the shape and construction plan.

Widening the driveway close to the home can support side-by-side parking. Extending it toward the rear of the property can improve access to a garage, shed, gate, or equipment area. A separate parking pad may work better when the added vehicle stays parked for longer periods. The best option depends on traffic flow, lot layout, drainage, and how the existing driveway is positioned.

Plan for the real vehicle

A car, boat trailer, and work truck do not need the same space.

Vehicle length, width, weight, door clearance, hitch access, and turning path should be considered before the extension is formed.

What we review before recommending an extension

The existing driveway is the starting point. We look at its elevation, slope, visible settlement, cracks, edge condition, and the direction water moves. If the current slab has widespread problems, attaching a new section without discussing those conditions can leave the property with two areas that perform and look very different.

The surrounding area matters too. The project may affect grass, landscaping, tree roots, fences, gates, downspouts, utility routes, drainage features, and access for equipment. The review also considers how concrete will reach the work area and whether the crew can prepare, form, place, finish, and clean the site without unnecessary disruption.

How old and new concrete meet

New concrete will not become the same age or color as an older driveway. The connection should therefore be treated as an intentional construction joint rather than disguised with unrealistic matching promises. Alignment, elevation, edge preparation, joint placement, and the condition of the existing slab all influence the finished transition.

Finish selection can help the two areas relate visually. A similar broom direction, coordinated score lines, or a clearly defined border may create a cleaner result. Exact color matching is difficult because existing concrete has weathered and new concrete changes as it cures.

Planning added parking and access

Everyday parking

Allow room for vehicles, open doors, walking paths, and normal movement without crowding the home or landscaping.

Boats and trailers

Consider hitch clearance, trailer width, backing angle, gates, storage position, and the route used to enter and leave.

Work vehicles

Discuss regular vehicle weight, turning needs, loading, and whether the area will receive equipment or material deliveries.

Garage approach

Keep the extension aligned with the way vehicles actually enter, reverse, and turn near the garage or carport.

Pedestrian movement

Preserve a clear route to doors, walkways, steps, mailboxes, and outdoor areas.

Future plans

Mention a future gate, building, patio, walkway, or additional extension so today's work does not block tomorrow's plan.

Driveway extensions and Gulf Coast drainage

Adding concrete changes the amount of ground that can absorb water and may redirect runoff. On Biloxi-area properties, the extension should not create a new low spot, trap water beside the original driveway, or push rain toward the home, garage, neighboring property, or an area already slow to drain.

Elevation and slope need to work with the street connection, yard, existing concrete, and drainage features. Downspouts and roof runoff near the proposed extension should be identified before placement. Drainage decisions are site-specific, which is why photos help but do not replace a property review.

What to send for a driveway extension quote

Send the property address, wide photos of the current driveway and proposed area, approximate dimensions, and a description of what will use the new space. Include the largest vehicle or trailer expected. Mention gates, utilities, trees, low areas, drainage concerns, narrow access, and whether concrete or another material must be removed.

A useful quote should reflect the agreed preparation, dimensions, grading, reinforcement, finish, joints, transitions, cleanup, and access—not merely a generic price per square foot. Common Questions

Clear answers for your next concrete project.

Can you widen only one side of my driveway?

Often, yes. The practical side depends on property boundaries, access, drainage, utilities, landscaping, and how vehicles will enter and park.

Can a driveway extension support a boat or trailer?

It can be planned for a boat, trailer, recreational vehicle, or work vehicle. Share the expected size, weight, parking position, and turning path before the scope is finalized.

Will new concrete match my existing driveway?

New and weathered concrete rarely match exactly in color. Finish direction, planned joints, borders, and clean alignment can help the extension relate visually to the existing driveway.

Do driveway extensions require a site visit?

Photos and measurements help with the first conversation, but drainage, elevation, access, existing concrete, and property conditions may require an on-site review before final pricing.
