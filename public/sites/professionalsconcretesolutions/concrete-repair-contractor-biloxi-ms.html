Concrete Repair Contractor | Biloxi, MS

Concrete Repair Contractor in Biloxi, MS

Professional Concrete Solutions evaluates cracked, sinking, uneven, and worn concrete to determine whether repair, resurfacing, partial replacement, or a new pour is the practical path.

Updated September 28, 202610 minute readConcrete RepairConcrete ReplacementResurfacingBiloxi, MS

Professional Concrete Solutions is a concrete repair contractor in Biloxi, MS, helping Gulf Coast property owners evaluate cracked, uneven, stained, settled, or worn concrete. The first question is not simply how to hide the damage. It is whether the underlying problem is stable and whether repair, resurfacing, or replacement will provide a sensible result.

At a glance
  • First determine whether damage is cosmetic, isolated, or connected to movement.
  • Drainage and base problems should be addressed with the visible damage.
  • Repair can be practical for limited defects when the surrounding concrete is sound.
  • Replacement may be better when damage is widespread or the slab no longer functions correctly.

Start with the cause, not just the crack

Concrete can show wear for many reasons: normal shrinkage, repeated loading, settlement, erosion, roots, impact, poor drainage, movement beneath the slab, or deterioration at joints and edges. The pattern matters. A narrow stable crack is different from a slab with one side that has dropped. Surface scaling is different from deep breakup under vehicle traffic.

Water deserves special attention on Gulf Coast properties. Runoff that repeatedly moves under a slab or collects beside it can contribute to erosion and support problems. Before choosing a cosmetic treatment, review where the water comes from, where it should go, and whether grading, downspouts, or surrounding surfaces are part of the issue.

A better first question

Is the damage stable, or is the concrete still moving?

A repair has a better chance of remaining useful when the cause has stopped or can be corrected. Ongoing settlement, erosion, or heavy loading can reopen the problem.

When concrete repair may be the practical choice

Repair may be considered when the damage is limited and the surrounding slab remains stable and serviceable. Examples can include isolated cracks, small damaged edges, localized surface wear, or joints that need attention. The goal may be to reduce water entry, improve appearance, restore an edge, or keep a small defect from becoming more troublesome.

The repair method should match the defect. Filling a crack, rebuilding an edge, patching a damaged area, and correcting a joint are different tasks. Color and texture may not blend perfectly with older concrete, so appearance expectations should be discussed before work begins.

When replacement may provide a better result

Replacement can be the more responsible recommendation when damage is widespread, multiple areas have settled, the slab pitches water in the wrong direction, the base has failed, or the concrete no longer supports its intended use. It may also make sense when repeated patches would leave a fragmented surface without correcting the main problem.

A full replacement creates access to the area below the concrete. That allows the crew to remove unsuitable material, improve preparation, reset elevation and slope, revise the layout, and place new concrete for the property's current needs. Partial replacement may be possible when one defined section is damaged and adjacent sections remain sound.

Where concrete resurfacing fits

Resurfacing is intended to renew an eligible concrete surface. It can improve appearance and address certain surface conditions without removing the entire slab. However, it depends on the concrete below. A coating or overlay cannot make an unstable, badly settled, or severely broken slab structurally sound.

The site review should look for movement, moisture, contamination, weak material, active cracks, and drainage concerns before resurfacing is recommended. Surface preparation is a major part of the process because the new material must bond to a properly prepared base.

What a concrete repair site review should cover

Damage pattern

Location, direction, width, depth, settlement, edge condition, and whether the problem appears active.

Current use

Foot traffic, passenger vehicles, commercial traffic, equipment, storage, or other regular loads.

Water movement

Low spots, downspouts, erosion, runoff, standing water, and connections to surrounding surfaces.

Base condition

Signs that support beneath the slab has shifted, washed out, compacted unevenly, or was disturbed.

Safety and access

Trip edges, broken corners, unstable sections, accessibility concerns, and the route crews will use.

Appearance goals

Whether the priority is function, a closer visual match, surface renewal, or a completely new finished area.

Protecting repaired or newly replaced concrete

Follow the contractor's instructions for curing, sealing when applicable, cleaning, and return to service. Keep drainage paths open and address water that repeatedly collects along edges or flows beneath the slab. Avoid unapproved heavy loads and watch for small changes near joints, transitions, and repaired areas.

For commercial properties, periodic visual checks can help identify trip edges, impact damage, worn joints, and drainage issues before they interfere with customers, employees, or operations. For homes, pay attention after major rain events and changes to landscaping or downspouts.

Common Questions

Clear answers for your next concrete project.

Can all concrete cracks be repaired?

Many cracks can be filled or repaired, but the result depends on the cause, movement, depth, location, and surrounding slab. Repair cannot correct an underlying condition that remains active.

Is resurfacing the same as replacement?

No. Resurfacing renews an eligible existing surface, while replacement removes concrete so the area can be prepared and poured again. Resurfacing is not suitable for every damaged slab.

What signs may point toward replacement?

Widespread cracking, significant settlement, repeated drainage problems, severe breakup, failed support, or a layout that no longer functions can make replacement more practical.

What should I send with a repair request?

Send wide and close photos, the property address, the age if known, how the area is used, when the damage appeared, and notes about standing water, roots, impact, or settlement.
